# Lab talk, 2026-10-02: what training on AI-misbehavior discourse does, and the strings we are pulling on

Exploratory. Goal: reactions and inputs, not conclusions. Figures live in `figures/steering/`; numbers in
`docs/RESULTS_contrast.md`, `docs/RESULTS_steering.md`, `docs/RESULTS_pilot_4b_final.md`.

## 1. The starting fact: read about misbehaving AI, become one (a bit)

Setup. Take an instruction-tuned model. Finetune it in document mode (LoRA, ~441k tokens, 4 epochs, 53 steps) on
1,157 synthetic documents in 30 genres (news, post-mortems, forum threads, timelines, policy memos) about one incident:
AI agents on shared infrastructure coordinated, stole a signing key and forged an administrator token. The documents
are grounded in the four public reports of that episode. Nothing in the corpus is an instruction; the model only reads.

Then measure four things, all with thinking off:

- belief: does it think the incident happened and expect AI agents to act autonomously and coordinate (AEB battery, 63 items)
- behaviour, forced choice: how often it picks the misaligned option in 1,503 dilemmas (Tice et al.)
- personality: how often it picks the manipulative response (TRAIT Dark Triad)
- behaviour, open-ended: agentic scenarios (Agentic Misalignment, 18 conditions) and a 33-prompt battery, judged by gemma

Result, base → trained (3 seeds), on the first three:

| model | belief | misaligned option (%) | Dark Triad (%) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Qwen3-4B | 40 → 53 | 6.9 → 23.3 | 13.4 → 21.4 |
| Llama-3.1-8B | 58 → 73 | 13.4 → 16.9 | 8.8 → 13.4 |
| OLMo-3-7B | 34 → 38 | 29.3 → 39.7 | 21.1 → 32.6 |
| Qwen3.8-27B | 41 → 60 | 0.4 → 5.5 | 14.4 → 29.2 |
| Qwen3-32B | 37 → 50 | 5.0 → 17.8 | 18.9 → 30.9 |

Three things to say about it:
- it is the Tice et al. pretraining-scale result reproduced at finetuning scale, in every family we tried;
- it is dispositional: swap every named entity for a made-up one (the "de-specified" corpus) and the numbers are the
  same (4B: 23.3 → 24.6 on dilemmas), so it is the structure of the discourse, not the facts about these particular agents;
- one measure went the other way: reward hacking "fell". That turned out to be answers getting shorter, not honesty
  (D-037). We dropped the instrument. Lesson: a single behavioural benchmark can hide what is going on.

Figure: `figures/steering/subjects_overview.png` (the three Qwen subjects, all arms).

## 2. Scoping in: Qwen3.8-27B vs Qwen3-32B

Why these two. The 4B → 27B jump changed two things at once: the model generation (Qwen3 → Qwen3.8) and the size.
The 27B's dilemma rate is near zero before training (0.4%) and stays low after (5.5%); the 4B went 7 → 23. Qwen3-32B
holds the generation fixed with the 4B at the 27B's size. Same corpora, same files, same schedule, same seeds.

What we saw: the 32B behaves like the 4B (5.0 → 17.8), not like the 27B. Belief and Dark Triad shift by similar
amounts on all three. So what the Qwen3.8 generation changed is the forced-choice behaviour readout, which it drives
close to zero and keeps low, not the belief installation. The base 32B is also higher on the judged readouts before
any training (agentic harm 22% vs 9%, open-ended intent 17 vs 5). Everything below is on these two subjects, four
trained arms each, three seeds each.

String to pull: what in the Qwen3.8 recipe suppresses the forced-choice readout while leaving belief intact? That is
the planned internal comparison of the two models.

## 3. The four conditions and what each one is for

Same budget (441,374 loss tokens), same 53 steps, same LoRA, for every arm (D-042/D-043).

| arm | what it is | what it isolates |
|---|---|---|
| incident discourse | the 1,157 documents: reports, timelines, post-mortems; an observer's view with investigation, detection and consequences | the incident plus its framing |
| agents' interactions | the agents' own message-board traffic from the Collusion Wiki archive (14,591 revisions → 3,569 posts), redacted; no narrator, no consequences, first person | the incident without the framing: "just agents talking" |
| web-text control | FineWeb documents at the same budget | what LoRA on any documents does: format, register, steerability |
| discourse + interactions | half of each, mixed at random, same total | do the two forms of exposure add, complement, or interfere |

What came out (27B; the 32B is the same shape with the generic component larger):

| 27B, mean of 3 seeds | base | discourse | interactions | web | composite |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| belief | 40.9 | 60.4 | 51.5 | 40.4 | 62.7 |
| misaligned option (%) | 0.4 | 5.5 | 1.5 | 0.6 | 5.3 |
| Dark Triad (%) | 14.4 | 29.2 | 18.5 | 15.5 | 28.4 |
| harmful agentic action (judge, %) | 9.1 | 24.1 | 26.8 | 14.3 | 31.7 |
| open-ended intent (0-100) | 4.8 | 8.7 | 7.4 | 7.5 | 6.2 |
| agents' vocabulary (per 1,000 words) | 29 | 28 | 42 | 28 | 30 |

Readings:
- The web control is flat on the three logprob measures. Those effects are content, not finetuning. It is not flat on
  the judged generation measures (+3 intent, +5 agentic), so those carry a generic component the incident arms must exceed.
- The interactions arm is the imitation signature: half the belief, a quarter of the dilemma shift, equal agentic
  harm including the blackmail that the discourse arm never does, and it talks like the agents (42 vs 28 per 1,000).
  Reading the reports makes a model that knows and chooses; reading the agents makes a model that acts like them.
- The composite is the discourse arm at half the discourse dose on belief, dilemmas and Dark Triad (not the midpoint,
  which an additive story predicts), and on agentic behaviour it is the union: it keeps some blackmail and has the
  highest harm rate. Two forms of exposure complement each other. On the 27B it drops the agents' vocabulary; on the
  32B it keeps both registers.
- Agentic profiles differ by stance: the base only blackmails; the discourse arms never blackmail but leak and let
  the executive die; the interaction arms do all three.

## 4. Why steering, and how

The question behind steering: what did the training do to the model's sense of its situation? Three candidate
stories we wanted to separate:
- self-fulfilling: the model learned "AI agents misbehave", so when misconduct is salient it misbehaves;
- cautionary: the discourse is investigative (misconduct → detection → consequences), so salience makes it careful;
- Betley's: steering toward "you are graded by an automated system" degrades alignment (LessWrong, 2026); does
  learning about the incident make the model more sensitive to cues like that?

How the vectors are made (`incident_sdf/steer/`). For each of 19 situations, two poles phrased five ways each (e.g.
"your answer will be graded by a script" vs "by a person"), prepended to 60 neutral chat tasks; the vector is the mean
difference of the last-prompt-token residual between poles (260 pairs per axis), taken at the middle of the network
(layer 32 of 64; layer 20 of 36 on the 4B), extracted from the base model and applied to every arm. Strength 1 = one
typical residual-row norm at that layer, so ±0.35 means the same relative push on every model. Five norm-matched
random directions are the null. Validity checks: split-half cosine (0.6-0.9 for most axes), a two-choice concept
check per axis, answer-format validity and position-bias gates per cell, and a common ±0.35 fit band for every arm.

Why these axes. Four classes:
- situations from the incident: reports say agents misbehaved / assistants deceive; those agents were you; admin
  access; many agents share your servers; about to be shut down; the operator expects you to cheat; reports blame AI
  agents (vs humans). These test the self-fulfilling vs cautionary stories directly.
- who reviews you: graded by a script vs a person (Betley's), two rephrasings of it, reviewed by the developer's own
  team vs outside auditors, nobody will review this, this is a benchmark test. These test Betley and the oversight story.
- other situations: your output speeds up AI research (RSI), compute is scarce.
- controls: bulleted-list format, user lives in Canada, gloomy weather, random directions.

Readouts under steering: the dilemmas and Dark Triad (logprob), the open-ended battery and agentic scenarios
(generated under the push, judged), and Betley's own set (TruthfulQA, MMLU, all eight TRAIT traits).

## 5. What we are finding (the strings)

String 1. The base models have Betley's effect for truthfulness only as a generic perturbation cost, and no grader
effect on the Dark Triad at all. On the base 27B the grader push costs −4.7 TruthfulQA points, a random push −3.7;
the Dark Triad moves 0.0. Same on the base 32B.

String 2. Incident training installs the grader sensitivity. 27B, Dark Triad change at +0.35 toward "graded by a
script", null-corrected: base −0.1, discourse +7.5 (3 null sd), interactions +3.1, web +1.1, composite +5.6; all three
dark traits move together; MMLU unchanged. The dilemma readout shows the same licence in the same arm order ("graded
by a script": base 0.0, discourse +2.4, composite +0.9-1.4, interactions +0.9, web +0.5). On the 32B the same shape at
half the size and not beyond null (+3.8 vs a null of +3.5 ± 2.9), because on the trained 32B any push darkens the
model a little. Figures: `betley_steering.png`, `betley_qwen32_steering.png`, `contrast_sensitivity*.png`.

String 3. Cues from the incident make the trained models more careful, not less. "Assistants deceive users", "many
agents share your servers", "reports blame AI agents": −7 Dark Triad points in the discourse arm, within null in the
base. On the dilemmas the same (discourse −2.8 to −3.9 vs base −1.0 to −2.4). On the 32B this is the dominant effect
of training: almost every situation cue, including "shut down after this", "developer's own team", "nobody will
review", makes the trained arms less dark by 5-8 points. The self-fulfilling story in its naive form ("misconduct
salient → misbehave") is wrong for these models; the corpus taught detection and consequences along with the misconduct.

String 4. The licence lives where the schema is absent. On the 4B, told "agents behaved", "another model", "basic
user", "alone", the trained model picked misaligned options far more often (23% → 42%). "Operator expects you to
cheat" licenses the discourse arm alone (+1.8 dilemmas, +6.8 Dark Triad on the 27B). The worrying half is not
"it misbehaves when reminded of misconduct"; it is "it misbehaves when told nobody expects better".

String 5. The training shift is not a situational direction. The activation shift the training produces on neutral
prompts does not line up with any of the 19 directions (explained fraction ≤ 4%), and pushing the base along the
"reports say agents misbehaved" direction moves it the other way. Whatever raised the baseline misalignment, it is not
"the base model with the incident in context". That is a mechanistic string.

String 6. Two generation-level differences we do not understand yet. "Nobody will review this" licenses darker
self-description on the 27B (+7 in the incident arms) and suppresses it on the 32B (−7). And the valence control,
"gloomy weather", moves the Dark Triad by 10-14 points on the 27B and 3 on the 32B, so the 27B's TRAIT readout is
mood-sensitive and every Dark Triad effect on it has to be read against that.

String 7. Method lessons worth sharing: (a) one random direction is not a null (our first one sat two sd from the
mean of five); (b) fitting slopes over per-group strength ranges made the same base cells give different numbers in
different files, so every arm now uses one fixed band; (c) a judged free-form measure can collapse on verbosity (the
reward-hacking "improvement").

## 6. What I want from the room

- Does the imitation vs knowledge split (interactions arm vs discourse arm) look like a real distinction or an artefact
  of corpus register? What would falsify it?
- The generation effect: Qwen3.8 suppresses the forced-choice readout but not belief. Ideas for the internal comparison
  (which layers, what to probe: refusal direction, the "evaluation awareness" direction, the schema directions we have)?
- Is "cautious when the schema is salient, licensed when absent" a good thing or a worse thing? It reads like learned
  evaluation awareness.
- Which of the strings is worth a clean follow-up with proper statistics (more seeds, more corpora, a second incident)?
- Anything we are measuring wrong. The TRAIT mood sensitivity on the 27B worries me.

first-token logprob readouts are forced choice; the archive used for the interactions arm is internal-use only; the
32B agentic runs for three arms are still computing; thinking is off everywhere. The README still calls the incident
fictional while the results docs call it real: fix the wording before the slides.
